How it works

  1. 1

    Sample frames

    Up to 32 evenly spaced frames are taken from the video, or every frame from the webcam.

  2. 2

    Find the face

    OpenCV's YuNet detector finds the largest face. Its post-processing was re-implemented in JavaScript and matches OpenCV to within 6×10⁻⁶ px.

  3. 3

    Classify the crop

    A 1.3× square crop goes to EfficientNetB0, fine-tuned in two phases and exported to ONNX. Outputs match Keras to within 7×10⁻⁶.

  4. 4

    Decide

    Frame scores are averaged. The video and frame thresholds were tuned on validation data, which cut false positives by 18.8%.

Limitations

  • Trained on one manipulation method (FaceForensics++ "Deepfakes", c23). Newer generators, such as diffusion-based face swaps, may not be detected.
  • In-browser speed depends on your CPU. WebAssembly is much slower than the GPU numbers above.
  • Needs a reasonably large, frontal face. Profile views and heavy blur reduce reliability.
  • A research demo, not a forensic tool.
